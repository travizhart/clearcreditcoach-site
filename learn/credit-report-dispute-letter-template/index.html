Credit Report Dispute Letter Template: What to Include

You can dispute online or by phone, but a letter gives you a clear record of exactly what you asked for and what you sent. Below is a free sample credit report dispute letter, a checklist of what to include, and tips for sending it.

By Travis Hart · Last updated October 4, 2026 · 4 min read

Key takeaways

  • A good dispute letter is short and specific: who you are, which item is wrong, why, and what you're asking the bureau to do.
  • Attach a copy of your report with the item circled, plus copies (not originals) of supporting documents.
  • Send a separate letter to each bureau that shows the error. Certified mail with a return receipt gives you proof of delivery.
  • There's no magic wording. "609 letters" and copied legal language don't get special treatment.

When a letter makes sense

Disputing by mail is a good fit when you have documents to include, when the error is complicated, or when you want a paper trail. The Consumer Financial Protection Bureau (CFPB) suggests sending mailed disputes by certified mail with a return receipt so you have a record that your letter arrived. If you prefer to dispute online, the same advice applies: be specific and upload your supporting documents.

What to include in a credit dispute letter

Based on the CFPB's guidance, your letter should include:

  1. Your contact information: full name, current address, and phone number. Bureaus may also ask for your date of birth and Social Security number to find your file. Include what the bureau's instructions ask for.
  2. Your report confirmation number, if your report has one.
  3. Each item you're disputing, with the company name and account number (or the last few digits).
  4. A clear explanation of why each item is inaccurate or incomplete.
  5. What you're asking for: that the bureau investigate and correct the information.
  6. A copy of the relevant part of your report with the disputed items circled or highlighted.
  7. Copies of supporting documents, such as statements or payoff letters. Never send originals.

Sample credit report dispute letter

Copy this template and replace everything in [brackets]. Delete any lines that don't apply. Use one numbered item per error.

[Date]

[Your full name]
[Street address]
[City, State ZIP]
[Phone number]
[Date of birth, if requested]

[Credit bureau name]
[Dispute mailing address from your report or the bureau's website]

Re: Dispute of inaccurate information. Report confirmation number: [number]

To whom it may concern:

I am writing to dispute the following information in my credit file. The items I am disputing are circled on the enclosed copy of my report.

1. [Company name], account ending in [last 4 digits]. This information is inaccurate because [explain clearly. Example: "the account shows a payment 30 days late in March 2026, but I paid on time on March 3, 2026. A copy of my bank statement showing the payment is enclosed."]

2. [Company name], account ending in [last 4 digits]. This information is [inaccurate/incomplete] because [explanation].

Please investigate these items and correct the information, as provided by the Fair Credit Reporting Act. Please send me the results of your investigation in writing.

Sincerely,
[Your signature]
[Your printed name]

Enclosures:
Copy of my credit report with disputed items circled
[Copy of bank statement dated ___]
[Copy of driver's license and proof of address, if requested]

The CFPB also publishes free sample dispute letters, one for the credit bureau and one for the company that supplied the information.

Writing to the company that reported the information

You can also dispute directly with the lender, card issuer, or collector that reported the information (the "furnisher"). Use the same structure: identify the account, explain what's wrong, attach copies of your proof, and ask them to investigate and correct what they report to the credit bureaus. Send it to the address the company lists for credit reporting disputes, which is often on your statement or the company's website. If the furnisher finds that what it reported was inaccurate, the FCRA requires it to notify the nationwide bureaus it reported to.

Tips for sending your letter

  • One letter per bureau. If the same error is on all three reports, send three letters.
  • Keep a full copy of everything you send, including the enclosures.
  • Use certified mail with a return receipt if you want proof of the delivery date. The investigation clock generally starts when the bureau receives your dispute.
  • Write down the date you mailed it and track the response. See what happens after you dispute for timelines.
  • Keep it to the facts. Short, specific letters are easier to investigate than long ones.

What to leave out

  • Anything that isn't true. Only dispute information you honestly believe is inaccurate or incomplete.
  • Mass form letters that dispute everything on your report. A bureau can decide a dispute is frivolous or irrelevant, for example if it doesn't include enough information to investigate.
  • Original documents. Send copies only.
  • Copied legal jargon. Plain English works.

What about "609 letters"?

You may see "609 letters" promoted online as a special template. Section 609 of the FCRA (15 U.S.C. § 1681g) gives you the right to get the information in your credit file. The rules for disputing inaccurate information and how bureaus must investigate are in section 611 (15 U.S.C. § 1681i). No particular letter format gets special treatment. What matters is that your dispute clearly identifies the item and explains why it's inaccurate or incomplete.

After you send it

The bureau generally has 30 days to investigate, sometimes up to 45. The outcome depends on what the investigation finds. Information that's verified as accurate can stay on your report, and results vary from person to person.
